Live from the HackAIton

Today isn't just a Floor 9 thing. The HackAIton runs across every floor of the building - teams from all over the company, building with AI. Here's what our team built, and what I took away from it.

Our team built Po Ruke ("at hand"): one app for everything you pay for by SMS today - parking, public transport, trains, travel insurance. Slovakia sends 10.3 million paid SMS a year and nobody remembers the codes. You tap, the app writes the right SMS with the right number, city and plate.

01 Native with great UX is easy now

"Native" used to mean a specialist iOS team, a specialist Android team, and months before the first real screen. That's no longer the bottleneck.

AI designed the screens. AI wrote the SwiftUI to match them, on top of ODS - One Design System. Real map with parking zones, real SMS composer, Slovak, English, German, Hungarian and Ukrainian. The hard part moved from "can we build it" to "do we know what good looks like".
02 I'm not an iOS developer

I'm a fullstack dev - web, backends, the odd script. Before yesterday I had never shipped a line of Swift. So I tried it myself, and it went great.

I needed to iterate fast and run a lot of agents at once, so I tried something new: firstmate. You talk to one agent - the first mate - and it runs the crew for you: spins up agents in parallel, gives each its own clean copy of the code, watches them finish and hands back finished work. No juggling ten terminal tabs. It was a blast.

My biggest limitation wasn't AI or Swift. It was my MacBook Air, which spent the whole day on the edge of melting.

03 Webview is a shortcut. Native is the road.

A webview gets you there faster - one codebase, ship it. But people feel it: the scroll that's almost right, the back swipe that doesn't follow your finger, the keyboard that jumps. Native UX is an order of magnitude better. And once building native costs about the same, the shortcut stops being one.

04 The part that stings

We don't have the source code of our own mobile app. So every change, even a small one, is a request, a queue and months of waiting.

This week a small team built a whole native app in a day. That gap - one day vs. months - is the real headline of this issue. It's not about the people on either side; it's about who holds the code.
05 And it was fun

Honestly, the best part. Oli, Zuzka, Adel and me, plus a few Claudes as full team members. Lots of "wait, it can do that?" moments, and zero of the usual "we will need a few sprints for that".

If you have something that's been stuck in a queue for months, reply. Maybe it's a 24-hour thing now.
