This issue - firstmate, and what comes after it

One thing on my mind

Last week I mentioned firstmate in a single paragraph. It deserves more, so this whole issue is about it.
01 Talk to one agent. Ship with a crew.

On a normal day I have 5 to 10 Claude Code sessions running at once. Some of them spin up their own subagents on top of that. It's powerful, but honestly, it's exhausting. Which terminal had the failing test? Who's touching which file? What was I even waiting for? Keeping all of that in my head is a job in itself.

Then I found firstmate, and it flips the whole thing. You talk to one agent - the first mate. It hires the crew, gives each agent its own clean copy of the code, watches them work, and only comes back when there's a real decision for you. And it all runs on top of Claude Code - no new interface, nothing extra to install and learn. The more I use it, the more impressed I am. It honestly feels like magic.

That's how Po Ruke was built at the HackAIton: several agents at once. One did the research, another built the dashboard, others took the remaining screens - parking, insurance, services. firstmate put it all together, and it did that really well.

The part I didn't expect: the specs it writes for its crew are excellent. Before an agent starts, the first mate hands it a clear brief - what to build, where it fits, how to check it's done. Good specs are half the job, and these are really good.

That's the shift: with very little human interaction, it produces things that are production quality - validated, tested, rebased on the latest code. My job became deciding what to build and what to merge.

Want to see it in action? Here's a longer video from the author of firstmate, building a full-stack app this way. Watch it all or just scroll through - both are worth it.

▶ Watch on YouTube - Kun Chen, "Building a Full Stack App with Agentic Engineering"

Kept honest: it's not a five-minute setup, it eats tokens, and six agents at once will cook a MacBook Air.

Is firstmate the endgame? Probably not. Anthropic just relaunched Projects in Claude Code: a coordinator splits the work across parallel agents, each on its own branch and its own copy of the code. Sounds familiar? That's the usual pattern - when something in the ecosystem works well, Anthropic builds it natively, and fast.

Question for you: how many agents do you run at once - and how do you keep track of them? Reply - I'm genuinely curious.
