## Guest: An engineer who doesn't understand engines
Technical education - check. Combustion engines - not a chance. Garden machinery and cars were never my domain.

Spring garden prep at my parents' place. Their rotavator won't start after winter. Every service shop in reach is fully booked. And Mom wants it fixed *today*, obviously.

## Lesson #1 - AI as an advisor, not an infallible god
I asked AI for a checklist: oil, spark plug, filters, carburetor. Bought the kit it pointed me to. It "confirmed" the engine name.

It didn't fit. Wrong generation of the same model - the AI never saw the actual label on the machine, and I never checked it against what it gave me.

**Takeaway:** AI is a huge help, but you still have to validate the details yourself. Skip that, and it happily walks you into a dead end - confidently.

## Lesson #2 - understanding beats fixing
Round two, I typed in the exact data straight off the manufacturer's label this time. AI walked me through it step by step - not just "replace this part," but *why*, and how the whole thing actually works.

The rotavator started. The garden got done. And I picked up a skill I didn't have on Monday.

**Takeaway:** feed AI the right inputs and it stops being a fixer and starts being a genuinely good teacher.

## Old t-shirt, new mindset
Small detail that turned into the whole point: the entire time I was doing this, I was wearing a Telekom t-shirt so old it's basically ventilated. Twenty years, several holes, still in rotation because "it still kind of works."

Same as "I don't know how to do this" or "that's not my job" - we hang on to old identities the same way we hang on to old t-shirts. Long past the point where either one is holding together.

Take the old t-shirt off. With AI in the loop, the line around "what's my job" moves a lot further than we think - and honestly, a new t-shirt wouldn't hurt either.

Thanks for reading - Viliam
