A quick word

Summer edition - lighter than usual, on purpose.

No frameworks, no big lessons this week. Just two little side quests I built at home for the family - the kind of thing that used to feel like a project and now just takes an evening. Here's what came out of it.
 
01 We wanted to go to Poland

It started at the kitchen table. The idea landed on Poland - a family road trip - so instead of the usual twenty tabs and a spreadsheet, I handed the whole thing to Claude to plan.

It built us a little site with options - routes, attractions, and hotels, all picked around what we needed with two small kids. My wife and I sat down, chose from what it offered, and over two evenings we had the whole trip planned. The kind of searching and combining that used to take ages - the AI did the heavy lifting.

We won't do every single thing on it, but the hotels and the route are locked in and I've got a set of ideas to dip into along the way. Fingers crossed it all turns out well.
 

Tap a stop up top and its whole plan unfolds. This is the version that survived a few rounds of "no, not that one."

02 Kids home sick, and I had to work

You know the situation. Kids home sick, a meeting you can't move. And at our place the tablet and TV aren't just lying around - screen time is the exception, not the default - so keeping them happily busy through a home-office day is genuinely hard.

So one day I just made them something - a simple app to learn their letters, and later put them into words. I prompted it, and a few minutes later I had a first version in my hands.

I tried it with them, saw what to fix, ran a few more prompts - and this is the result. It kept them busy and happy for a good while. And I got to work. Everybody won.

03 The summer point

Neither of these is work. Neither is impressive. That's the charm of it. When building something small gets this easy, it becomes your default reaction to little everyday problems - just for the people at home instead of at the office.

That's the summer edition. It's summer, the stakes are low, go build something silly for someone you like. Then tell me about it over coffee - bring a laptop, I'll show you mine.
