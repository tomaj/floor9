Presentations are HTML now. Attachments were never built for that.

This issue - DeckLink, in numbers

Why it exists

Almost every presentation I make today is HTML - AI writes it. The problem was sharing it in Teams or Outlook. As an attachment, the other person has to download the file and then open it. Too complicated, and on an iPad or a phone it often doesn't work at all.

So since June we have DeckLink: upload the presentation, share a link, and anyone can open it right away, on any device. If you've ever clicked "Read online" at the bottom of FLOOR 9, you've already used it.

1 in 4 is made with AI

Most decks are still uploaded - made somewhere else, often also with AI. But 65 of them were created right inside DeckLink, with its AI editor.
65 172
■ Created with AI - 27% Uploaded - 73% ■

An estimate: a deck counts as "with AI" if its first version had an AI session within 10 minutes.

People write real briefs, not one-word commands: 925 prompts, about 580 characters each on average. That's a paragraph, not "make it nicer".

And decks don't get uploaded once and forgotten. Half of them (120 of 237) have more than one version, about three on average. The record is 32 versions - same link the whole time.

API calls from other tools

The API part is how this newsletter gets online: when an issue is ready, a script pushes it to DeckLink and the "Read online" link at the bottom points there.

Built by Adam Majerčák, Robert Blaško and me - 280 commits since 1 June, a new "What's new" release roughly every week. It runs on Cortex, our internal app platform.
